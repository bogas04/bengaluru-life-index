BENGALURU LIFE INDEX · OUR VISION

Know your locality.
Make the case for change.

For concerned citizens and groups that care about their localities: gather evidence, understand local problems, and make a clear case to civic bodies. Use data to debate, protest, raise concerns and follow what changes.

A civic evidence tool · capabilities below are aspirations
WHO THIS IS FOR

People paying attention to their neighbourhoods.

Concerned residents, locality-focused groups, resident associations, and people who document the city through walks and videos—such as BengaWalk on YouTube. The primary purpose is to help them get usable data for civic action. Choosing a place to live is another way to use that same evidence.

Build an evidence-backed concern

Identify a location and problem. Bring together mapped conditions, dated observations, photographs and relevant comparisons. Record what is known, what is estimated and what still needs checking.

Use it in public and with civic bodies

Take an understandable evidence pack into a discussion, public meeting, representation, petition or protest. Explain the gap, why it matters, the responsible body and the specific action being requested.

Follow the response and the result

Keep the concern, supporting evidence, requested action and official response together. Revisit the location and compare conditions over time, so a conversation can lead to a documented improvement.

THE CORE LOOP

Data → issues → improve

Make conditions visible. Check the evidence behind each gap. Identify who can act, then measure whether conditions improve.

01 · OBSERVE

Data

Bring services, streets, costs and environmental conditions onto one ward map. Show where the evidence is strong, old or missing.

02 · UNDERSTAND

Issues

Identify possible gaps, verify them with observations, and distinguish a real problem from incomplete mapping.

03 · ACT & REVISIT

Improve

Help citizens present a confirmed issue to the responsible civic body, request a specific action and track its response and the outcome over time.

BETTER CIVIC CONVERSATIONS

Start with a claim. Ask for the evidence.

We want conversations about wards to be more data oriented, less driven by vague impressions, and less framed around religious, caste or other community identities. Discuss specific conditions, access to services and decisions that can improve them.

Make the claim specific

“This ward is bad” becomes a question about a particular condition: where footpaths are discontinuous, which streets flood, or how far residents are from a usable transit stop.

Make the evidence inspectable

Show the source, date, geographic coverage and method. Compare like with like. Separate measured conditions from estimates, and say when the available data cannot support a conclusion.

Make disagreement productive

Ask what observation would confirm or change a claim. Resident experiences can identify gaps for investigation; document and verify them. Evaluate proposed actions by their expected outcomes and revisit those outcomes after implementation.

QUESTIONS WE WANT TO ANSWER

From a city overview to a useful next step.

01 / MY WARD

What are the problems in my ward?

See gaps in basic services, uncomfortable walking routes, waterlogging, road conditions, noise and green space. Each issue should include evidence, its age and confidence, and room for residents to correct it.

02 / ACCOUNTABILITY

Who do I contact for which problem?

Match an issue and location to the responsible agency or ward team. Provide verified contact details, reporting channels, what information to include, and how to follow up. Contact ownership and details must be checked regularly.

03 / FINDING A HOME

Which ward fits the life I want?

Compare places using a real monthly budget, daily destinations, public transport and personal priorities. Show the trade-offs behind the score, then narrow the choice to localities and streets.

04 / COMPARISON

What separates my ward from the best fit?

Compare the same factors, weights and data snapshot side by side. See raw values and the largest gaps, including where a service exists but isn’t accessible. “Best” means best for the chosen priorities.

05 / OUR SHARED FUTURE

What should Bengaluru look like in 15–20 years?

Use a bird’s-eye view to imagine better access across the whole city, including wards that need the most attention. Start with the desired everyday experience, choose measurable outcomes, and work backward to the investments and actions needed now.

A SIMPLER WAY TO EXPLORE

Organize layers around everyday life.

As the map grows, group its controls into a few familiar themes. Keep individual factors available within each group, with search and a clear view of the factors currently shaping the index.

Civic & essentials

Healthcare, schools, fire services, waste, water, public toilets and local service gaps.

Entertainment & leisure

Cafés, cinemas, malls, libraries, parks and places to spend time together.

Work & daily journeys

Office destinations, metro, buses, rail, transfers and the walking part of a commute.

Environment & comfort

Green cover, lakes, noise, industry, odour and conditions during rain.

Housing & affordability

Rent, total monthly expenses and the cost of reaching daily destinations.

Streets & safety

Footpath width and continuity, crossings, potholes, waterlogging and usable routes.

A 15–20 YEAR HORIZON · 2041–2046

Start with the city we want.
Work backward to today.

Imagine a Bengaluru where everyday essentials are easy to reach, public transport connects people to opportunity, walking feels pleasant, and neighbourhoods remain usable through the rains. These are aspirations to translate into measurable targets and assess against a documented baseline.

Define the destination

Agree on outcomes: access, affordability, comfort, safety and resilience. Set explicit thresholds and include the wards with the poorest current access.

Trace the path backward

Translate the long-term picture into milestones, ward priorities, infrastructure needs, responsible teams and funding questions.

Begin, measure, adjust

Identify near-term improvements. Record a baseline, revisit conditions and check whether the change improved people’s actual experience.

HOW WE WANT TO BUILD

Useful, transparent and grounded in place.

Evidence before a verdict

Missing data is visible. Mapped counts, estimates and measured conditions are distinct. A proxy can suggest where to investigate; it cannot establish a problem on its own.

People define what matters

Personal indices help with individual choices. Civic priorities should use explicit criteria and evidence of unmet needs. Check who is underserved instead of relying on ward averages or assumptions about a community.

Improve access, not just rankings

A city succeeds when everyday conditions improve across wards. Track outcomes and gaps alongside which places rank highest.

Keep the next step close

Move from a layer to a clear explanation, then to a verified contact or improvement proposal. Make the evidence and progress easy to revisit.

WHERE WE ARE TODAY

The ward map is our starting point.

The prototype already supports amenity layers, personal weighted indices, top-five rankings and source documentation. Evidence packs for civic concerns, issue histories, verified contact routing, official response tracking, side-by-side ward comparisons, street-level journey quality and long-term scenario planning are future work.
